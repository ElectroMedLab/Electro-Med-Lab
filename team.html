Dr Akhil Jain

Lecturer in Drug Delivery · Division of Pharmacy and Optometry, School of Health Sciences · Faculty of Biology, Medicine and Health

Biography

Dr Akhil Jain is Lecturer in Drug Delivery in the Division of Pharmacy and Optometry at the University of Manchester and an EPSRC Fellow. He received his PhD (2017) in Materials Physics and Nanoscience from UNAM (Mexico) and UCSD (CA, USA), having earlier completed an M.Tech in Biotechnology at the Vellore Institute of Technology, India, and a B.Tech in Biotechnology at Amity University, Uttar Pradesh, India. He remained at UNAM's Centre for Nanoscience and Nanotechnology as a Guest Lecturer from 2017 to 2018 before moving to the UK to take up a postdoctoral position in Bioelectronics at the School of Pharmacy, University of Nottingham, from 2018 to 2024, working on wireless bioelectronic approaches to cancer therapy. In May 2024 he joined the University of Manchester as Lecturer in Drug Delivery, where he now leads a research programme in bioelectronic medicine, supported by an EPSRC Open Fellowship that established the Electro-Med group and runs from 2026 to 2031.

Dr Chithrambary Reghukumar

Dr Chithrambary Reghukumar joined the Electro-Med Lab in September 2026 as a Research Associate, funded through an EPSRC Open Fellowship awarded to Akhil. She completed her PhD in Biomaterials from the University of Manchester on the Dean's Doctoral Scholarship, supervised by Prof Sarah Cartmell and Prof Jonny Blaker, with research focused on designing electrical stimulation bioreactors for adipogenic differentiation of human mesenchymal stem cells. She holds an integrated master's in chemistry from ISERC, Visva‑Bharati University, and gained further research experience through internships at BARC, working on lanthanide doped luminescent nanomaterials for nuclear waste immobilisation, and at IISc Bengaluru, on nanoparticle systems for photothermal cancer therapy.

In her current role, Chithra focuses on quantum enabled nanomaterials for electric field sensing, working closely with Dr Louise Natrajan at the University of Manchester and Dr Eva Hemmer's lab at Ottawa, Canada.

Outside the lab, she enjoys travelling and crafts, and is active as a ResLife Advisor, organising community events for students.

Maryam Ebrahimnia

Maryam joined the University of Manchester in 2026 as an MRC DTP PhD student in Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences. She holds a Doctor of Pharmacy (PharmD) degree from Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences. Her research experience focuses on the development of novel drug delivery systems, including nanofibers, niosomes and in situ gels.

Her current PhD research, under the primary supervision of Dr Akhil Jain, involves developing nano‑bioelectronic platforms to overcome drug resistance in glioblastoma and non‑small cell lung cancer. Ultimately, this approach aims to increase the sensitivity of tumour cells to chemotherapy and radiotherapy and improve overall treatment efficacy.

Outside the lab, Maryam enjoys practising Pilates, reading, and spending time with friends.

Komal Ranpise

Komal joined the University of Manchester in October 2025 as a PhD researcher in Advanced Biomaterials for Neuroscience, funded by the University's Bicentenary PhD Award. She holds a BSc in Microbiology from Savitribai Phule Pune University and an MSc in Nanotechnology and Regenerative Medicine from University College London. Before her MSc, she gained laboratory experience through internships in animal and stem cell culture in Pune.

Her PhD research, supervised by Prof Aline Miller, Prof Alberto Saiani and Dr Akhil Jain, investigates how external electrical stimulation influences peptide self assembly and the properties and organisation of peptide hydrogels. The research aims to develop aligned peptide nanofibrous hydrogels capable of guiding directional neuronal growth, with potential applications in peripheral nerve regeneration.

Outside research, Komal enjoys reading mythological stories, classic romance and murder mysteries, listening to Indian retro music and ghazals, and dancing to Bollywood music.

Zhouchong (Benjamin) Wang

Zhouchong (Benjamin) Wang joined the University of Manchester in October 2025 as a PhD researcher, having previously completed his master's degree in clinical medicine at Shanghai Jiao Tong University. He brings a dual background as a clinical hepatobiliary surgeon and a medical specialist in clinical studies for innovative drugs within the pharmaceutical industry.

Working under the supervision of Dr Christos Tapeinos, Dr Mohamed Elsawy and Dr Akhil Jain, his research focuses on localised drug delivery systems for glioblastoma treatment. He aims to combine his clinical insights and industry experience with advanced biomaterial platforms to enhance the translational potential of targeted intracranial therapies.

Outside the laboratory, Benjamin enjoys hiking, travelling and reading detective novels.
